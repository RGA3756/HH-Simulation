The bucket and the open drain

Think of the membrane as a bucket (an electrical capacitor). To fire a spike, positive charge has to fill it from the resting level up to the peak. The smallest amount that could possibly do the job is Qmin = Cm × (Vpeak − Vrest).

Sodium entering the cell is the tap. But the potassium channels are a drain that opens while the tap is still running. Water pours in and straight out again. Everything that came through the tap is QNa. The part that flowed in while the drain was open is the overlap.

r = QNa / Qmin says how many bucketfuls you paid for per bucketful you needed. Efficiency is η = 100% / r. The cell pays for every 3 sodium ions with one ATP molecule, when its pump pushes them back out.

Warming makes every gate open and close faster (×3 per 10 °C). The tap shuts sooner after the peak, so less sodium is wasted and r falls. The spike also gets smaller, and above about 29 °C its peak no longer reaches 0 mV.
